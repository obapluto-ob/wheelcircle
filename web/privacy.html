PRIVACY NOTICE · VERSION 2026-11-18

What the wheel records.

A savings circle only works when the books are honest. This notice explains exactly what THE WHEEL records, why it is needed, who can see it, and the controls that protect it. It is written to match what the service actually does in code.

1. What we collect

  • Account details: your name, phone number, legal name, national ID digits and, if you choose to give it, your date of birth.
  • Identity record: your ID number is converted to a one-way SHA-256 hash with a fixed prefix before it is stored. The raw digits are not kept on the account. We keep the last four digits and the legal name you typed so you and the reviewer can see what was submitted. The hash is used only to stop the same ID holding two accounts.
  • Passkeys: the public key and credential ID generated by your device. The private key never leaves your device and cannot be read by us.
  • Circle activity: seats, contributions, late status, guarantees, bonds, patience shares, payouts, disputes and the ledger entries behind them.
  • Payments: amounts, references and outcomes from Paysasa, including checkout and M-PESA status. We never receive your M-PESA PIN, your SIM credentials, or full card data.
  • Security signals: hashed request addresses for rate limiting, session records, and sign-in timestamps.

2. Why we hold it

  • To run the circle — record contributions, calculate patience shares and fees, settle turns, and show every member the same books.
  • To meet payment and identity rules — one account per person, one seat per circle, and a verifiable identity trail for the money that moves.
  • To prevent fraud and abuse — duplicate IDs, replayed payments, reversed M-PESA transactions, and attempts to take a pot without contributing.
  • To answer you and the law — support, disputes, and lawful requests from Kenyan authorities.

3. Who sees what

  • Other circle members see your display name, seat, trust tier, paid or late status for each turn, and your guarantor link. They see your phone number only in masked form.
  • The organizer sees the same circle view and can record covers from the guarantee fund, their wallet or a guarantor. The organizer does not see your full number or your ID.
  • Our reviewer sees the account behind an identity check when a name and ID do not match, and the details needed to release or reverse a payment.
  • Paysasa receives the payment amount, reference and the number needed to collect or pay out, as a licensed payment provider.
  • We do not sell your data, do not run advertising, and do not share it with third parties for their own marketing.

4. How long we keep it

Account and ledger records stay for as long as your account is open and afterwards for as long as financial-record and dispute rules require. Raw identity input is cleared from the sign-up step once the account exists; the account itself holds only the hash, the last four digits and the legal name you typed. Security logs and rate-limit counters are short-lived.

5. The security measures behind it

  • Passkey sign-in only. There is no password to phish. The server checks the challenge, the page origin and the passkey’s RP ID on every sign-in.
  • Session cookies are HttpOnly and Secure over HTTPS, with SameSite protection, plus a double-submit CSRF token for state-changing requests.
  • Origin allowlisting. The API answers only the configured app origin and origins you deliberately trust, so a second host cannot ride on your session.
  • Provider secrets stay server-side. The Paysasa key and webhook secret live in server configuration, never in the page you load.
  • Signed webhooks. Paysasa events are verified with an HMAC-SHA256 signature before any money is credited, amounts are re-checked server-side, and replays are ignored.
  • Server-side rules. Contribution amounts, late fees, fees, bonds and settlement maths are calculated on the server. The browser only displays them.
  • Rate limits and hashed keys. Sign-in, sign-up and payment attempts are limited using hashed identifiers, so limits work without storing the raw address in the limit key.
  • Output escaping and a content security policy reduce the chance that a member-supplied name or note can run code in another member’s page.
  • One-way identity storage. IDs are compared by hash, not by keeping a list of numbers.

6. Your choices and rights

  • Ask for a copy of what we hold about you.
  • Correct your name or phone if they are wrong.
  • Ask us to close your account. Records that a circle or the law still needs stay, and you will be told which ones.
  • Ask how a decision about your identity status was reached.
  • See the guarantee links for your circle and ask the organizer to record a change before a turn falls due.

Requests go through your circle organizer to the operator. We answer within a reasonable time and tell you if a request cannot be met and why.

7. Age

Seats are for members aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly keep accounts for anyone under 18; if one is found, it is closed and the data is handled under this notice.

8. Changes

If this notice changes, the version and date at the top change with it. A material change asks you to accept the new version before your next circle action. The version you accepted is stored on your account with the date and the site you accepted it from.

9. Law

We handle personal data in line with the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019, and the rules of the payment provider we settle through. Kenyan law governs this notice.

Plain version Your circle needs to know who paid, who waited and who won. We keep that record honest, keep your ID as a one-way hash, keep your phone masked to other members, and keep the payment secrets on the server. Nothing else is collected for its own sake.
